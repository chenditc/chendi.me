BEWARE SOFTWARE BRAIN

有一种思维方式，把整个世界看成一系列数据库——然后试图用代码去控制它们

Nilay Patel 把这种思维叫做 software brain。Marc Andreessen 2011 年写下「软件正在吞噬世界」的时候，他就是 software brain 的化身。而 AI 把这种思维方式涡轮增压了——因为它让更多人能写出更多软件，让每一种生意都能用软件自动化自己的一大块。

问题在于：不是所有东西都是生意，不是所有东西都是循环。整个人类经验无法被塞进数据库。这就是 software brain 的极限——也是为什么人们讨厌 AI。

Today on Decoder, I want to lay out an idea that's been banging around my head for weeks now as we've been reporting on AI and having conversations here on this show. I've been calling it software brain, and it's a particular way of seeing the world that fits everything into algorithms, databases and loops — software. Software brain is powerful stuff. It's a way of thinking that basically created our modern world. Marc Andreessen, the literal embodiment of software brain, called it in 2011 when he wrote the piece "Why software is eating the world" as an op-ed in The Wall Street Journal. But software thinking has been turbocharged by AI in a way that I think helps explain the enormous gap between how excited the tech industry is about the technology and how regular people are growing to dislike it more and more over time.

THE NUMBERS

ChatGPT 有 9 亿周活用户——但超过一半的美国人认为 AI 弊大于利

NBC 的民调显示，AI 的好感度比 ICE（移民执法局）还低，仅略高于伊朗战争和民主党。Quinnipiac 发现超过 80% 的人对 AI 感到「非常担忧」或「有些担忧」，只有 35% 的人对它感到兴奋。

最讽刺的是 Z 世代：他们用 AI 用得最多，但对 AI 的感受也最负面。Gallup 发现只有 18% 的 Z 世代对 AI 抱有希望（去年还是 27%），而感到愤怒的比例从 22% 升到了 31%。

In fact, the polling on this is so strong, I think it's fair to say that a lot of people hate AI, and that Gen Z in particular seems to hate AI more and more as they encounter it. There's that NBC News poll showing AI with worse favorability than ICE and only a little bit above the war in Iran and the Democrats generally. That's with nearly two thirds of respondents saying they used ChatGPT or Copilot in the last month. Quinnipiac just found that over half of Americans think AI will do more harm than good, while more than 80 percent of people were either very concerned or somewhat concerned about the technology. A recent Gallup poll found that only 18 percent of Gen Z was hopeful about AI, down from an already-bad 27 percent last year. NOT A MARKETING PROBLEM

Sam Altman 说 AI 需要「更好的营销」——但 9 亿人每天都在用，这不是营销问题

OpenAI 花了 2 亿美元赞助 TBPN 播客，因为他们觉得这能让人们更喜欢 AI。Altman 亲口说：「如果 AI 是一个政治候选人，它会是历史上最不受欢迎的候选人。」因此他认为 AI 需要更好的营销。

Patel 的回应很直接：你没法通过广告让人们忽略自己的亲身体验。每个人都已经在 Google 搜索里看到了 AI 概述，在社交媒体上看到了大量 AI 生成的垃圾内容。这不是信息不对称，这是 software brain 和普通人生活之间的根本断裂。

It feels like someone just needs to say this clearly, so I'm just going to do it. AI doesn't have a marketing problem. People experience these tools every single day! ChatGPT has 900 million weekly users, trending to a billion, and everyone has seen AI Overviews in Google Search and massive amounts of slop on their feeds. EVERYTHING IS A DATABASE

Zillow 是房子的数据库，Uber 是车和乘客的数据库——一旦你这样看世界，你就觉得控制数据就能控制一切

这种思维方式确实很强大。我们生活的大部分确实运行在数据库上，一堆重要的公司就是围绕维护和提供数据库访问而建立的。YouTube 是视频的数据库，The Verge 的网站是故事的数据库。

但 数据库不是现实。Elon Musk 和 DOGE 进入政府后，第一件事就是接管一堆数据库——然后他们撞上了一个不可否认的事实：政府不是软件，人不是计算机。任何真正运行过数据库的人都知道，数据库总会在某个时刻跟现实脱节。到那时候，我们通常会去修改数据库，而不是修改世界。

So what is software brain? The simplest definition I've come up with is that it's when you see the whole world as a series of databases that can be controlled with the structured language of software code. Like I said, this is a powerful way of seeing things. So much of our lives run through databases, and a bunch of important companies have been built around maintaining those databases and providing access to them. Zillow is a database of houses. Uber is a database of cars and riders. YouTube is a database of videos. The Verge's website is a database of stories. You can go on and on and on. Once you start seeing the world as a bunch of databases, it's a small jump to feeling like you can control everything if you can just control the data. But that doesn't always work. Here's an example: Elon Musk and DOGE showed up in the government, and the first thing they did was take control of a bunch of databases. And they ran into the undeniable fact that the databases aren't reality, and DOGE ended in hilarious failure. It turns out software brain has a limit — the government isn't software. People aren't computers, and they don't live in automatable loops that can be neatly captured in databases.

SOFTWARE BRAIN MEETS LAWYER BRAIN

代码用结构化语言控制机器，法律用结构化语言控制社会——这种令人陶醉的相似性让所有人都上了当

Patel 曾经当过律师，他妻子现在还是律师。他发现 software brain 和 lawyer brain 之间的重叠深得令人着迷。两者的核心都是：相信用结构化的形式语言思考，就能让现实世界发生改变。代码有库和先例，法律有判例法；工程师复用代码库，律师引用判例——两者都试图用形式化语言去引导复杂系统的行为。

I also spend all of my time at work talking to tech people. And so over time, I've learned that the overlap between software brain and lawyer brain is very, very deep. Alluringly deep. If the heart of software brain is the idea that thinking in the structured language of code can make things happen in the real world, well, the heart of lawyer brain is that thinking in the structured legal language of statutes and citations can also make things happen. Hell, it can give you power over society. There are other commonalities. Both software development and the law depend heavily on precedent. We have a body of case law in this country, and we use it over and over again to help us resolve disputes, just like software engineers have libraries of code that they turn to repeatedly to build the foundations of their products. The similarities run deep: at the end of the day, both lawyers and engineers do their best to use formal, structured language to guide the behavior of complicated systems in predictable and potentially profitable ways.

BUSINESS LOOPS

在商业世界里，software brain 确实有效——因为商业本身就已经是软件了

任何看起来像「代码对数据库说话」的重复性业务流程，都在被 AI 接管。这就是为什么 Anthropic 一直在死磕企业客户，OpenAI 也在转向 B 端。因为现代商业本身就是软件：收集数据、分析数据、基于数据采取行动，然后循环。企业还能控制自己的数据，能要求所有数据库互通。

咨询公司不会真正研究你的业务来提高效率——他们做的是制作 PPT 来向董事会和股东证明裁员的合理性。大型咨询公司擅长这个，现在他们只需要用 AI 来生成那些 PPT。他们已经在这么做了，裁员也已经开始了。

Any business process that looks like code talking to a database in a repetitive way is up for grabs. That's why Anthropic has been so relentlessly focused on enterprise customers, and it's why OpenAI is now pivoting to business use. There's real value in introducing AI to business, because so much of modern business is already software: collecting data, analyzing it, and taking action on it over and over again in a loop. Businesses also control their data, and they can demand that all their databases work together. You don't hire a big consulting firm to actually come in and study your business and make it more efficient. You hire them to make slide decks that justify layoffs to your board and shareholders. Big consulting firms are great at this, and now they're just going to generate those decks with AI. They are already doing this and the layoffs have already begun.

THE LIMIT

不是所有东西都是循环。人们不渴望自动化。

Patel 自称是「重度智能家居患者」——他家的灯光、窗帘和温控都以几十种方式自动化了。但 Apple、Google 和 Amazon 花了十多年试图让普通人关心智能家居自动化，结果是：他们就是不关心。

AI 不会改变这一点。大多数人并没有在收集关于自己一切的数据。就算收集了一点，这些数据也散布在各种系统里——Gmail 里的邮件、iMessage 里的短信、Outlook 里的日程、Peloton 里的健身记录。这些系统互相不通，也许永远不会通，因为没有理由让它们通。而让人们去连接这一切只会让他们感到恐惧。

Regular people don't see the opportunity to write code as an opportunity at all. The people do not yearn for automation. I'm a full-on smart home sicko; the lights and shades and climate controls of my house are automated in dozens of ways. But huge companies like Apple, Google and Amazon have struggled for over a decade now to make regular people care about smart home automation at all. And they just don't. AI isn't going to fix that. Most people are not collecting data about every single thing that they do. And if they're collecting any at all, it's stored across lots of different systems — your email in Gmail, your messages in iMessage, your work schedule in Outlook, your workouts in Peloton. Those systems don't talk to each other and maybe they never will, because there's no reason for them to. Asking people to connect them all freaks them out.

THE DOOMED ASK

让人适应电脑是注定失败的——电脑应该适应人

Ezra Klein 去了硅谷，发现那些 AI 从业者们正在拼命让自己变得对 AI 可读：开放邮件、日历、消息，让 AI 在后台持续运行，建立关于你的偏好和模式的持久记忆。这听起来很赛博朋克，但 Patel 说得很清楚：让人把自己变成数据库，这是一个注定失败的想法。

这个要求大到无法想象有什么回报能让它值得——更何况科技行业还在不停地说 AI 会消灭所有工作、需要全面重新思考社会契约、最新的模型可能导致灾难性的网络安全问题。这只有在你患上了 software brain 的时候才说得通。对其他所有人来说，AI 不过是一个贪婪的垃圾怪兽。

I've reviewed a lot of tech products over the past decade and a half, and all I can tell you is that it is a failure when you ask people to adapt to computers. Computers should adapt to people. Asking people to make themselves more legible to software — to turn themselves into a database — is a doomed idea. It's an ask so big that I can't imagine a reward that would make it worth it for anyone, even if the tech industry wasn't constantly talking about how AI will eliminate all the jobs, require a wholesale rethinking of the social contract and — oops — also the latest models might cause catastrophic cybersecurity problems that might lead to the end of the world. Does this sound like a good deal to you? Can you market your way out of this? This only makes sense if you have software brain — if your operative framework is to flatten everything into databases that you can control with structured language. For everyone else, AI is just a demanding slop monster. It's a threat. And so the tech industry is rushing forward to put AI everywhere at enormous cost — energy, emissions, manufacturing capacity, the ability to buy RAM — and locked into the narrow framework of software brain without realizing they are also asking people to be fundamentally less human. They then sit around wondering why everyone hates them. I don't think a couple haircuts are going to fix it.

PRECEDENT = LIBRARIES

判例法之于律师，就像代码库之于工程师——两者都在复用前人的结构化语言

这种相似性深入骨髓：法律和代码都严重依赖先例。一个国家有一整套判例法体系，律师反复引用它来解决争端，就像软件工程师反复依赖代码库来构建产品的基础。两者最终都在做同一件事——用形式化的结构语言，去引导复杂系统的行为朝可预测（且可能盈利）的方向走。

这种令人陶醉的相似性总是让人犯错。人们不断试图像给计算机发指令一样给整个社会发号施令。Patel 最喜欢的例子是那些 Facebook 转发帖，坚称 Mark Zuckerberg 无权发布人们的照片——仿佛在终端里输入一行命令就能改变现实。

There are other commonalities. Both software development and the law depend heavily on precedent. We have a body of case law in this country, and we use it over and over again to help us resolve disputes, just like software engineers have libraries of code that they turn to repeatedly to build the foundations of their products. This intoxicating similarity between law and code trips people up all the time. People are constantly trying to issue commands to society at large like it's a computer that will obey instructions. There are examples of this big and small — my favorite are those Facebook forwards insisting Mark Zuckerberg does not have the right to publish people's photos. Honestly, I look at these, and I think it would be great if the law was actually code. Maybe things would be more predictable. Maybe we'd feel more in control.

AMBIGUITY IS THE HEART

法律的核心不是确定性——而是模糊性

你不能拿一个案件的事实加上法条，就确定地预测结果。正是模糊性让律师成为律师，也让人们讨厌律师——因为总能找到灰色地带，总能论证另一方。这就是为什么检察官最后去当辩护律师，监管者最后去为大企业打工。

密歇根州前最高法院首席法官 Bridget McCormack 在 Decoder 上推销过一个完全自动化的 AI 仲裁系统。她的论点是：人们觉得传统司法系统太不公平，所以他们会接受一个自动化系统给出的更差的结果，只要他们觉得自己被倾听了。Patel 说：这就是纯粹的 software brain——相信我们能强迫现实世界像计算机一样运作，然后让 AI 给它下指令。

But law isn't actually code, and society and courts aren't computers. I have to remind our fairly technical audience on Decoder and at The Verge all the time that the law is not deterministic. You simply cannot take the facts of a case, the law as written, and predict the outcome of that case with any real certainty, even though the formality of the legal system makes people think it works like a computer — that it's predictable. But at the end of the day, it's actually ambiguity that's at the very heart of our legal system. It's ambiguity that makes lawyers lawyers. Honestly, it's ambiguity that makes people hate lawyers because it's always possible to argue the other side, and it's always possible to find the gray area in the law. That's why prosecutors end up working as defense attorneys and why our regulators tend to end up working for big corporations. Bridget McCormack, who used to be the chief justice of the Michigan Supreme Court, was on Decoder a few months ago pitching a fully automated AI arbitration system. Her argument to me was that people perceive the traditional legal system to be so unfair that they will accept a worse outcome from an automated system as more fair as long as they feel heard. And if there's one thing AI can do, it's sit there and listen all day and night. I don't know if any of that is correct or even workable, but I do know software brain, and that is pure software brain: the idea that we can force the real world to act like a computer and then have AI issue that computer instructions.
