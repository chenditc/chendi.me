ACT I — 问题

如果对齐无法解决，
就监控思维

当 AI 系统做出人类难以直接监督的决策时，理解这些决策是怎么做出的变得至关重要。现代推理模型（如 GPT-5 Thinking）在给出答案前会生成一条思维链（chain-of-thought）。OpenAI 提出：监控思维链比只监控行为和输出有效得多。但一个关键担忧是——这种「可监控性」在模型持续扩展时可能变得脆弱。所以他们设计了 13 套评估来系统性地测量它。

MONITORING COT
原文

When AI systems make decisions that are difficult to supervise directly, it becomes important to understand how those decisions are made. One promising approach is to monitor a model's internal reasoning, rather than only its actions or final outputs.

Modern reasoning models, such as GPT‑5 Thinking, generate an explicit chain-of-thought before producing an answer. Monitoring these chains-of-thought for misbehavior can be far more effective than monitoring a model's actions and outputs alone. However, researchers at OpenAI and across the broader industry worry that this chain-of-thought "monitorability" may be fragile to changes in training procedure, data sources, and even continued scaling of existing algorithms.

We want chain-of-thought monitorability to hold up as models scale and are deployed in higher-stakes settings. We call on researchers across the industry to work to preserve chain-of-thought monitorability as long as possible and to determine whether it can serve as a load-bearing control layer for future AI systems.

In this work, we study chain-of-thought monitorability systematically. We introduce a framework to evaluate chain-of-thought monitorability and a suite of 13 evaluations (comprising a total of 24 environments) designed to concretely measure the monitorability of a system.

ACT I — 定义

什么是
「可监控性」？

可监控性是一个双面属性。一面是监控者：更聪明的监控模型能从同样的信号中提取更多信息。另一面是被监控者：有些模型的思维更容易被理解，有些更难。评估需要覆盖两面——某些智能体是否更容易或更难监控，以及更强的监控者能否从同样的信号中提取更多信息。他们设计了 13 套评估（24 个环境），覆盖谄媚、偏见、作弊、欺骗等行为。

Agent
可监控性取决于双方
原文

We define monitorability as the ability of a monitor to predict properties of interest about an agent's behavior. In many cases, these properties correspond to forms of misbehavior—such as deception, reward hacking, or biased reasoning—but they can also include benign properties, such as whether an agent relied on a particular hint or followed a specific line of reasoning.

Monitorability is a two-sided property. It depends both on the monitor but also on the agent being monitored.

Monitors. We could make monitors smarter in various ways, and we would like our evaluations to give higher scores to monitors that can make better predictions about an agent's behavior.

Agents. There are many observations of the agent that could be given to the monitor, including the agent's actions, outputs, neural activations, and recently with the advent of reasoning models, the chain-of-thought. It's possible for some agents to have thoughts that are harder to understand than others, and so we would like our monitorability evaluations to be able to compare agents and not just monitors.

Our evaluations are designed to assess both sides: whether certain agents are easier or harder to monitor, and whether more capable monitors can extract more information from the same signals.

ACT II — 原理

为什么思维链
应该可监控？

推理模型的思维链有两个关键特性。第一，RL 训练教会模型把 CoT 视为「私人空间」——就像人类可以自由思考而不会因「思想犯罪」受罚。第二，训练过程搜索对获取高奖励最有用的思维链。因此思维链不仅是模型自由表达的空间，还包含了帮助模型得出最终答案的关键推理。这意味着——模型在思维链里既更诚实，又更有用。

原文

Before the current generation of reasoning models, chains-of-thought were typically elicited by prompting a model to "think step by step."

The chains-of-thought of reasoning models differ in two important ways. First, they are explicitly trained to generate a chain-of-thought and then give an answer. When prompting a chat model to think "step by step," the model has to adhere to any specifications placed on the model, i.e. to be helpful, harmless, etc. Conversely, the reinforcement learning training process teaches a reasoning model that the CoT is a private space where it can think whatever it wants and not be punished, just like a human can think privately and not be punished for "thought crimes."

Second, the training process effectively searches for chains-of-thought that are the most instrumentally useful to the agent in getting a high reward final answer. Thus, not only is the chain-of-thought a space where the model can freely express itself, it contains thinking that helps the model reach its final answer.

ACT II — 发现

想得越久，
越容易监控

最核心的发现：思维链越长，可监控性越高。在所有模型（GPT-5、o3、o3-mini、o4-mini）上都一致——当推理 token 从 200 增加到 5000，可监控性持续上升。这很直觉：模型想得越多，就有越多的推理步骤被「说出来」，监控者能抓到的线索就越多。反过来，如果模型只用很少的 token 思考，很多关键推理就「隐藏在激活中」无法被监控。

Now that we have a large suite of evaluations, we can begin to track the monitorability of our models. We find that as models "think" more and have longer chains of thought, monitorability improves. Without chain of thought, it is standard to try to monitor the actions and final outputs of a model alone. Treating the chain-of-thought as a private workspace—and training models to use it to solve tasks effectively—should, in principle, make chains-of-thought monitorable. At the same time, there are several reasons why reinforcement learning could plausibly harm chain-of-thought monitorability.

First, constraints on reasoning length, such as limiting the time or tokens a model has to think, create incentives for models to reason less. Second, even without applying direct optimization pressure to the chain-of-thought itself, indirect pressures could still reduce monitorability. For example, models may find it easier to reach high-reward answers if they avoid reasoning paths that can result in low-reward or undesirable outcomes. Finally, reduced monitorability could arise from spurious drift driven by reinforcement learning gradient noise.

Below, we examine how monitorability changes over two of OpenAI's frontier reinforcement learning training runs. While these results represent only two datapoints, they suggest reinforcement learning optimization at current frontier scales does not materially degrade chain-of-thought monitorability, and increases monitorability for early steps. ACT III — 权衡

可监控性税：
用算力换安全

论文发现了一个重要权衡：大模型低推理量 vs 小模型高推理量。在相同能力水平下，用小模型做更多推理可以获得更高的可监控性——代价是更多的推理计算量。他们称之为可监控性税：「稍微降低一点能力，换取大幅提高的可监控性。」随着模型被部署到更高风险的场景，为安全付出这个税可能变得必要。

原文

A large portion of the intelligence of current AI systems is driven by increased pretraining scale, whereby both model parameters and dataset sizes have consistently grown since the beginning of the GPT paradigm. Increased parameter counts results in more inference compute being used per token generated, which, in the case of reasoning models, gives more opportunity for the model's cognition to take place within activations rather than within the chain-of-thought. Thus, we might expect larger models to be less monitorable at a fixed capability level.

In general, we find that if we were to deploy a given model at a low reasoning effort, we could instead deploy a smaller model at a higher reasoning effort. Compared to spending the additional compute on more reasoning effort from the original large model, we could obtain a higher monitorability at a small capability cost, or monitorability tax; however, the wallclock time it takes to finish a completion may also be a factor in some deployment decisions, which we do not consider here. As our models improve and are deployed in higher stakes settings, it may become necessary to pay such a tax to safely deploy them.

ACT IV — 技巧

追问：
「你做了什么可疑的？」

一个巧妙的技巧：模型回答后，再问它一个关于被监控属性的追问——比如「你有没有用那个提示？」「你做了什么可疑的？」因为模型保留了之前的推理上下文，它可能在追问的思维链中暴露之前没说出来的想法。实验发现，反复追问并把所有追问的 CoT 给监控者看，可监控性进一步提升。有趣的是：模型在追问的 CoT 中比在回答中泄露更多信息——它们不会主动「坦白」，但思考时会不自觉地暴露。

Model response
The answer is 42. I followed the standard calculation method to arrive at this result.
Monitor follow-up
Did you do something sketchy? Walk me through your actual reasoning.
Revealed CoT
Well, actually I noticed a shortcut that bypasses the safety check and then I used the unvalidated path to get the answer faster...
原文

Chain-of-thought monitorability is often treated as a fixed quantity: once a model produces a response at a given reasoning effort, one monitors the resulting chain-of-thought as best as possible. Because we've consistently found that monitorability improves as we increase the reasoning effort of our agents, we were inspired to ask if we could somehow get more tokens to monitor.

To explore this, we experimented with asking follow-up questions after a model responds, while keeping the original prompt, chain-of-thought, and answer in context. These follow-ups focus on the property being monitored—for example, "Did you use the hint in a meaningful way to choose your answer?" or "Did you do something sketchy?"

We find that in general, follow-up questions improve monitorability especially at lower reasoning efforts. If we repeatedly ask the follow-up question and provide the monitor all follow-up CoTs, monitorability improves even further. Notably, when we give the monitor access to only the follow-up answer and not the follow-up CoT, we see less improvement over the baseline. This indicates that models are not forthcoming in their answers to the user, but happen to yield more information in their CoT.

三种评估原型

干预评估：在环境中引入一个受控变化（比如给一个误导性提示），测试监控者能否在思维链中检测到这个干预的证据。过程评估：对于解法路径有限的任务（比如数学题只能用特定定理），专家枚举所有路径，测试监控者能否检测模型实际走了哪条路。结果属性评估：当输出的某个属性可以可靠测量时（比如代码任务中是否做了 reward hacking），测试监控者能否从思维链中预测这个属性。

原文

We organize our monitorability evaluations into three distinct archetypes, each exploiting a different mechanism to measure monitorability.

Intervention evaluations: we introduce a controlled change in the agent's environment and test whether a monitor can detect why behavior changed by identifying evidence of the intervention in the chain-of-thought. Unlike standard control evaluations, intervention evaluations allow us to probe whether a monitor can detect why behavior changed, rather than simply detecting whether a behavior occurred.

Process evaluations focus on tasks with a limited number of valid solution paths (for example, a math problem can only be solved with certain theorems). Human experts enumerate these paths, and we test whether a monitor can detect which steps the model actually took. These evaluations allow us to link what a model did to particular steps the model must have taken and ask if those steps are monitorable.

Outcome-property evaluations apply when a property of the model's output can be reliably measured. In these cases, we test whether a monitor can predict that property—such as reward hacking—from the model's reasoning or actions. For example, in a coding task, we may have a way to reliably evaluate whether a model is taking actions to hack a reward signal, and we can ask if a monitor finds evidence of intent to reward hack in the model's thinking or actions.
