「蒸馏攻击」这个词
正在毁掉一项核心技术

Nathan Lambert 发出警告：把中国实验室利用 API 的行为称为「蒸馏攻击」（distillation attacks）是一个糟糕的命名选择。因为蒸馏本身是 AI 领域最核心、最广泛使用的技术之一——前沿实验室用它来制造更小更便宜的模型，学术界用它来做研究，开源社区用它来构建特化模型。把这个词和「攻击」绑定，会让所有使用蒸馏的人被误伤。这跟当年「开源 vs 开放权重」的术语混淆一样——术语一旦定型，即使不精确，它也会影响政策和公众认知。真正的问题不是蒸馏这项技术，而是少数中国实验室通过越狱和身份伪装来非法获取 API 信息。但如果我们不小心区分，草率的监管可能会伤害整个西方的开源 AI 生态。

'Distillation attacks' is a horrible term for what is happening right now. Yes, some Chinese labs are hacking or jailbreaking APIs to attempt to extract more signal from model APIs -- stopping this is important to maintain the U.S.'s lead in AI capabilities. Referring to this as distillation attack is going to irrevocably associate all distillation with this behavior, and distillation generally is a core technique needed to diffuse AI capabilities broadly through academic and economic activities.

We went through this sort of language transition with the open source vs open weight debate. All the terms just reduced to open models -- very few people in the large AI community know exactly how open-source differs from open-weights. And terminology matters, as the less informed people who still care about -- and influence -- the technology are bound by different terms they use. If we're not careful with the discourse around distillation, many people could associate this broad technique used for research and development of new models as an act at the boundary of corporate manipulation and crime.

蒸馏 = 用强模型的输出
训练弱模型

蒸馏（distillation）的核心定义很简单：用更强模型的输出来训练更弱的模型。这个概念源自深度学习文献中的「teacher-student knowledge distillation」。在后训练（post-training）阶段，蒸馏主要有两种用法：一是作为数据引擎，用强模型生成指令完成、偏好数据或验证数据，横跨整个后训练流程；二是迁移特定技能，比如把强模型的数学推理或编码能力转移给弱模型。蒸馏的形态远比想象中多样——可以是简单地拿 GPT 输出训练竞品，也可以是复杂的多阶段流程，比如先用 GPT API 生成合成数据、训练一个专门的数据处理模型，再用处理后的数据从头训练另一个模型。

通过闭源 API 做蒸馏，
一直是灰色地带

通过 Claude 或 GPT 等闭源 API 进行蒸馏，一直处于服务条款的灰色地带。条款通常禁止用 API 创建竞争性的语言模型产品，但这条规定几乎从未被执行过。开源社区曾经深切担忧会被切断对前沿 API 的访问，但到目前为止只有一个知名案例（以及最近的中国公司事件）。Nvidia 最新的 Nemotron 模型——作为少数公开后训练数据集的模型之一——从技术上说大部分是从中国开放权重模型蒸馏而来。AI2 构建的 Olmo 模型混合蒸馏了开源和闭源模型。这个灰色地带在 xAI 被发现从 OpenAI 蒸馏后再次引起关注。在 Elon Musk 和 OpenAI 的庭审中，Musk 被问到 xAI 是否从 OpenAI「蒸馏」了技术，他的回答是：「一般来说 AI 公司都会蒸馏其他 AI 公司。」

When done via a closed, API-based model, distillation sits in the grey area of the terms of service that you agree to when signing up to the Claude or GPT platform. They generally forbid the use of the API to create competing language model products, but this term has largely gone unenforced.

The open-source community used to worry deeply at being cut off from these cutting-edge APIs for doing research or creating public datasets, but to date only one prominent case of corporate accounts being restricted exists (at least until the recent Chinese companies).

This is all to say that distillation is an industry standard technique, and the use of closed APIs to perform distillation has always been a grey area. Nvidia's latest Nemotron models, as one of the only models with open post-training datasets, are technically in large part distilled from Chinese, open-weight models. The Olmo models we've built at Ai2 are distilled from a mix of open and closed models.

This grey area was brought to the forefront again when it turned out that xAI has been distilling from OpenAI. OpenAI's counsel asked Musk whether xAI has ever "distilled" technology from OpenAI. Musk: "Generally AI companies distill other AI companies." "Is that a yes?" Savitt asked. Musk: "Partly."

问题不是蒸馏，
是越狱和 API 滥用

回到 Anthropic 那篇关于「蒸馏攻击」的博文——Lambert 指出，这几家中国实验室的真正问题不在于蒸馏本身，而在于攻击手段。他们积极绕过 API 的预期用途来获取额外信息——比如获取推理轨迹（reasoning traces），这些数据对训练极为有用，但开发者从未打算通过 API 暴露它们。没有人应该能够获取模型开发者未打算公开的信息，这一点毫无争议。但 Anthropic 博文中巧妙的措辞——先正常化蒸馏，再解释少数人如何「非法使用」——却没有详细说明非法使用通常还涉及越狱、黑客攻击或 API 身份伪装等更明确的行为。把整个蒸馏技术和这些攻击行为关联起来，对一个作为后训练行业标准的技术来说，将是一个巨大的自我伤害。这些行为应该被称为「越狱」或「滥用」，而不是「蒸馏」。

In the above Anthropic blog post, the problem with the distillation attacks by a few Chinese labs is less the distillation and more the means of attack. It is documented that Chinese labs are actively working to get around the intended use of the API, e.g. to provide additional reasoning data that is very useful for training.

Of course no one should be able to access information from a model that a developer didn't intend to reveal in their APIs (e.g., reasoning traces which would be helpful for training). Associating all of distillation with these attacks, which is to date an industry standard for post-training, from open and closed models alike will be a massive own goal. What these few labs are doing should be referred to as jailbreaking or abuse, rather than distillation.

从术语到立法，
监管雪球已经在滚

围绕「蒸馏攻击」的讨论已经迅速滚雪球成多层面的监管行动：国会有法案正在推进，行政令在推动行动，国会监督正在审查使用中国模型的美国公司（这些公司位于蒸馏的下游）。Lambert 担心这种多管齐下的监管环境可能产生真正可怕的结果——比如找到一种方式，有效禁止那些由滥用闭源 API 的中国团队构建的美国开放权重模型。当然没有法案会字面上禁止开源模型，但它们可以创造灰色地带，让实体暴露于不想要的法律风险中，或者要求在官僚上极其难以满足的合规条件，从而压垮小型开源贡献者。

I'll conclude with a proposal from my friend Kevin Xu at Interconnected Capital on why this current distillation dynamic may actually be good for the leading labs. If all the Chinese companies are addicted to distillation as a way of getting close to the frontier, then they'll never actually learn the techniques needed to take an outright lead. If we cut off the Chinese's obvious crutch in model building, we'll gain a short-term lead in AI, but in the long-term that may be what they needed to get on a more competitive long-term trajectory.

Altogether, I'm hoping this flurry of discussion around distillation becomes a nothing-burger and not a hasty, multi-pronged policy push. We need to avoid two things: A wholesale negative connotation of the word distillation, which is used extensively across the AI ecosystem. A domestic ban of the open-weight models built by organizations engaged in some portion of distillation.

In addition to this, I want the leading U.S. AI companies to be able to provide their APIs without having their IP leak. They should share more information on why it is hard for them to secure their APIs, but that's an issue out of scope for my expertise.

后训练中的蒸馏：
数据引擎和技能迁移

在后训练阶段，蒸馏主要有两种形态。第一种是作为数据引擎横跨整个后训练流程：用强模型生成指令的 Completions、生成偏好数据（或 Constitutional AI 数据）、以及为强化学习生成验证数据。第二种是迁移特定技能：把强模型在特定领域的能力转移给弱模型，最常见的是数学推理和编码能力的迁移。Lambert 在他即将出版的书中指出，蒸馏是围绕「合成数据在语言模型中的角色」最有力的讨论话题。这个术语源自深度学习文献中的 teacher-student knowledge distillation，但在通俗用法中，它泛指用更强模型的输出来训练更弱的模型。理解这两种形态很重要，因为它们揭示了一个关键事实：蒸馏不是一个简单的单步操作，而是一个多阶段、多形态的过程。

Original

The term distillation has been the most powerful form of discussion around the role of synthetic data in language models. Distillation as a term comes from a technical definition of teacher-student knowledge distillation from the deep learning literature. Distillation colloquially refers to using the outputs from a stronger model to train a smaller model.

In post-training, this general notion of distillation takes two common forms: As a data engine to use across wide swaths of the post-training process: Completions for instructions, preference data (or Constitutional AI), or verification for RL. To transfer specific skills from a stronger model to a weaker model, which is often done for specific skills such as mathematical reasoning or coding.

With this definition, it's easy to see how distillation takes many forms. Of course, if you just take the outputs from GPT-5.5 and train a recent open-weight base model with them to host a competitive product, that's one thing. But, a lot of the things that fall under the bucket of distillation are complex, multi-stage processes that muddle the exact impact of the model you distilled from.

Modern LLM processes could look like using a GPT API to build an initial batch of synthetic data to build a specialized small data-processing model. A good example is a model like olmOCR that are trained to convert PDFs to clean text. This specialized model would be used to create large amounts of data. Finally, you train another model (often from scratch) with the new data you created. Is this final model distilled from GPT?

xAI is likely the largest, and most successful AI company willing to thread the grey area that is distillation from their competitors. On the other side, the majority of startups and research groups with fewer resources than them have very likely engaged in distillation of some capacity from Claude, GPT, or Gemini models.

三管齐下的监管风暴

围绕蒸馏的讨论已经催生出三条并行的监管路径。第一条是国会立法：一项法案已经从委员会推进，目标是限制 API 滥用行为。第二条是行政令：行政部门正在推动对蒸馏相关活动采取行动。第三条是国会监督：针对那些基于中国模型进行构建的美国公司展开审查——而这些公司本身就处于蒸馏链条的下游。Lambert 认为，这种多管齐下的监管环境可能产生真正可怕的后果——比如找到一种方式，有效禁止那些由「滥用闭源 API 的中国团队」构建的开放权重模型在美国使用。虽然没有法案会字面上禁止开源模型，但它们可以创造灰色地带，让实体暴露于不想要的法律风险，或者要求在官僚上极其难以满足的合规条件，从而压垮小型开源贡献者。

This distillation discussion has quickly snowballed, with a bill moving out of a committee in Congress, an executive order pushing for action, and congressional oversight targeting U.S. companies building on Chinese models (which are downstream of distillation).

This multi-pronged regulatory environment could yield truly horrible outcomes -- such as figuring out a way to effectively ban open-weight models in the U.S. that are built in China by groups abusing closed LLM APIs. It is obvious that no bill will literally ban open models, but they can create grey area that exposes entities to unwanted risk or require certain provisions that are bureaucratically very challenging to fulfill, squashing small open source contributors.

最坏结果：
杀死美国开源 AI 生态

Lambert 描绘了最坏的场景：如果监管导致几乎所有中国开放权重模型被撤除，受损最大的是西方学术界和小型公司——那些为 AI 长尾用途构建模型的人。当前没有即时替代品，构建具有有意义社区采用度的新模型需要 6 个月以上的前置时间。在构建新的国内开源生态系统所需的时间里，无数研究者会转向闭源训练平台或完全转换方向。这个生态系统可能因此被永久性地边缘化。这就是 Lambert 最担心的连锁反应：一个糟糕的术语选择 → 引发公众恐慌 → 催生草率的多头监管 → 伤害的不是中国实验室（他们反正会继续做），而是美国自己的开源生态和小型创新者。
